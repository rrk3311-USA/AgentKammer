Volume 1 - Licensing + Brokerage Law

1. Licensing Basics

Plain English

A real estate license gives someone legal permission to perform brokerage activity for another person for compensation.

  • Listing property
  • Selling property
  • Buying property for another
  • Leasing property
  • Negotiating real estate terms
  • Collecting compensation for real estate services
Exam rule: If someone performs real estate services for another person and expects compensation, they generally need a license.
Trap: An owner can sell their own property without a license. Once they sell, lease, negotiate, or advertise property for others for compensation, licensing law is triggered.
"Licensing exists because the state wants to protect the public from untrained people handling major property transactions."

Mini quiz

  • Can an owner sell their own house without a license? Yes.
  • Can an unlicensed person collect a referral fee for bringing a buyer? Usually no.
  • Can a salesperson operate independently? No.

2. Salesperson vs Broker

Salesperson

Licensed but must work under a sponsoring broker.

Broker

May operate independently, supervise salespersons, hold escrow, and receive commissions.

Key logic: The broker is the responsible legal operator. The salesperson acts through the broker.
Trap: If an answer says the salesperson can directly collect commission from the client, be suspicious.
"The salesperson is licensed, but the broker is the legal command center."

3. Sponsoring Broker

Rule: A NY salesperson must be associated with a sponsoring broker to practice real estate brokerage.

Broker supervises

  • Advertising
  • Agency disclosure
  • Transaction files
  • Commission payment
  • Escrow handling
  • Compliance
Agent Kammer note: Brand, website, and media presence are fine only if brokerage advertising rules and broker/team disclosure rules are followed. Safe language: "Licensed real estate salesperson affiliated with [brokerage]."

4. Compensation Rules

Salespersons are generally compensated through their broker, not directly by the public. Referral fees involving licensed activity usually flow broker-to-broker.

Exam rule: Commission rates are always negotiable. There is no legal fixed rate.
NY note: Kickbacks that violate RESPA or license law are danger zones. Personal "thank you" checks from clients to salespersons are still compensation-structure problems.
Trap: "Buyer pays salesperson directly after closing." Usually wrong.
"The client may feel they hired the agent, but the brokerage structure controls compensation."
Mini quiz
  • Can a salesperson accept a direct closing gift check outside the broker? Usually no.
  • Are commissions fixed by NY law? No - negotiable.

5. Continuing Education

Licenses expire and must be renewed. Continuing education keeps agents legally current.

Exam rule: A license does not last forever. The state can require renewal, education, and updated compliance.
NY note: Confirm current CE hours and required topics with DOS for your renewal cycle. Fair housing and agency topics are commonly emphasized. Prefer DOS / your course over random blogs.
Trap: Assuming inactive knowledge stays valid forever.
Mini quiz
  • Do licenses renew? Yes.
  • Should CE hours be verified from DOS materials? Yes.

6. Advertising Rules

Big idea

Real estate advertising must not mislead the public.

Ads should disclose / show

  • Licensed status / broker affiliation as required
  • Accurate property information
  • No false claims
  • No discriminatory language

Dangerous claims

  • "Guaranteed appreciation"
  • "Safest neighborhood"
  • "Perfect for families only"
  • "#1" without support
  • "No broker needed" when brokerage rules apply
Safe: "Market data suggests..."
Unsafe: "This will definitely go up."

7. Trust Funds / Escrow

Money held for others must be handled carefully: earnest money, security deposits, and other escrow funds.

Exam rule: Trust money must not be mixed with personal or business operating funds.
Trap: Commingling = mixing. Conversion = using trust money for personal/business use. Conversion is severe.
NY note: Residential deals are often attorney-escrow heavy, but exam still tests broker trust-account logic and public protection.
"If the money is not yours, treat it like evidence in a glass case."
Mini quiz
  • Mixing escrow with operating account? Commingling.
  • Using escrow to pay rent? Conversion.

8. Disciplinary Action

DOS can discipline licensees for conduct that harms the public or violates license law.

  • Fraud / misrepresentation
  • Discrimination
  • Mishandling escrow
  • Unlicensed activity / false advertising
  • Failure to disclose
  • Acting outside broker supervision
Exam rule: When in doubt, choose disclosure, broker supervision, and public protection.
Trap: "My client told me to" does not legalize an illegal act.
"The exam sides with the public, not the hustle."
Volume 2 - Agency + Representation

9. Agency Relationships

Agency is the legal relationship where one person represents another.

  • Principal / client: the person represented
  • Agent: authorized to act
  • Customer: non-client party who gets honesty and fair dealing, not full loyalty
Exam rule: Client gets fiduciary duties. Customer gets fairness, not loyalty.
NY note: Seller's agent, buyer's agent, broker's agent, dual agent, designated sales agent.
Trap: A buyer chatting with the listing agent often thinks they have an agent when they are only a customer.
"A client gets loyalty. A customer gets fairness."

10. Fiduciary Duties

OLD CAR = Obedience · Loyalty · Disclosure · Confidentiality · Accounting · Reasonable care
  • Obedience: follow lawful instructions
  • Loyalty: client interest above agent interest
  • Disclosure: tell client material information
  • Confidentiality: protect client secrets
  • Accounting: safeguard money and documents
  • Reasonable care: competence and diligence
Trap: Do not obey illegal instructions, including discriminatory directions.

11. Seller Agency

A seller's agent represents the seller and owes fiduciary duties to the seller.

Exam rule: Help the seller sell under lawful terms. Disclose material facts as required. Be honest with buyers without owing them loyalty.
Trap: Treating a touring buyer as a client when only seller agency exists.
"The listing agent can be friendly to a buyer and still represent only the seller."
Mini quiz
  • Buyer asks for seller's bottom line. Disclose freely? No.
  • Must listing agent disclose known material defects? Yes.

12. Buyer Agency

A buyer's agent represents the buyer and owes fiduciary duties to the buyer.

  • Protect max price, urgency, motivation, and negotiation strategy
  • Help locate, evaluate, and negotiate property
Trap: Revealing that the buyer "will pay whatever it takes."
"Buyer agency exists so the buyer is not wandering the market as an unprotected customer."

13. Dual Agency

Dual agency means one broker represents both buyer and seller in the same transaction.

Exam rule: Requires informed consent / disclosure. Not automatically illegal.
NY note: NY allows disclosed dual agency with informed consent. Undivided loyalty is limited.
Trap: "Dual agency is always illegal" is usually wrong.
"Dual agency can exist, but everyone must know and agree."

14. Designated Agency

Designated agency means the broker assigns different agents in the same firm to represent different clients in one deal (designated sales agents).

Exam rule: Still requires disclosure and consent where required.
NY note: Gives each client a more direct representative than one person trying to dual-represent alone.
Trap: Treating designated agency as a secret workaround.

15. Agency Disclosure

Consumers must know who the agent represents before they are misled.

NY note: Real Property Law §443 agency disclosure forms (seller/buyer and landlord/tenant) are used at first substantive contact. Get acknowledgment and keep records as required.
Also: Property Condition Disclosure (PCD): for most 1-4 family home sales, NY sellers must provide a PCD statement or credit the buyer $500. This is separate from agency disclosure.
Exam rule: Disclose agency early and clearly.
"I can assist you, but I represent the seller unless we enter a buyer agency relationship."

16. Broker Supervision

The broker supervises licensed activity: contracts, ads, agency forms, escrow procedures, negotiations, and recordkeeping.

Trap: A salesperson "doing their own thing" is usually the wrong answer.
"Independence in marketing is not independence in license law."
Volume 3 - Fair Housing + Ethics

17. Federal Fair Housing

Fair housing law prohibits discrimination in housing-related transactions and advertising.

  • Federal classes: race, color, religion, sex, national origin, disability, familial status
  • Prohibited: refusal, different terms, discriminatory ads, steering, blockbusting, redlining, false availability
Trap: Thinking only refusal to sell is illegal. Advertising and steering count too.
"Fair housing is not politeness. It is federal law with exam teeth."

18. New York Protected Classes

Big idea

New York Human Rights Law adds protections beyond federal law (examples often tested: age, marital status, military status, sexual orientation, gender identity/expression, lawful source of income, and more).

Memorize the current DOS / course list for your exam year. If a question involves identity, family status, disability, source of income, or neighborhood preference - slow down.

19. Steering

Meaning

Guiding people toward or away from neighborhoods or buildings based on protected class.

Bad examples

  • "You would probably be more comfortable in this area."
  • "This building has people like you."
  • "This neighborhood has good families."
Show properties based on objective criteria: price, location, size, transit, amenities.
"Steering is when the agent becomes the filter instead of letting the client decide from lawful options."

20. Blockbusting

Blockbusting induces owners to sell by suggesting protected-class changes will hurt values or "neighborhood character."

Exam rule: Illegal. Panic-selling pitches are red flags.
Trap: "Sell now before the neighborhood changes."
"If the pitch uses fear of who is moving in, it is blockbusting."

21. Redlining

Redlining denies or restricts loans, insurance, or services based on location tied to protected-class patterns.

Exam rule: If a lender refuses service because of neighborhood demographics, think redlining.
Trap: Confusing redlining (capital/services) with steering (agent guidance of people).
"Steering moves people. Redlining starves places of capital."

22. Reasonable Accommodation

A reasonable accommodation changes rules or policies so a person with a disability has equal use and enjoyment of housing.

Exam rule: Assistance animals are not treated the same as ordinary pets. No-pet policies often must yield to lawful accommodation requests.
Trap: Charging a standard pet fee for an assistance animal as if it were a pet.

23. Advertising Compliance

Ads may not express preference, limitation, or discrimination based on protected class.

  • Risky: "adults only," "no children," "Christian building," "perfect for singles," "safe neighborhood"
  • Safer: "near subway," "two bedrooms," "elevator building," "doorman," "close to park"
Exam rule: Describe the property and amenities. Do not describe the preferred resident.
Trap: Positive-sounding bias is still bias.

24. What Not To Say

  • Safety: Do not say "This is safe." Point to public crime data resources.
  • Schools: Do not say "This is a good school district." Point to school performance resources.
  • Demographics: Do not describe who lives there by race, religion, family status, or similar categories.
  • Appreciation: Do not say "This will go up." Say historical market data is not a guarantee of future performance.
Volume 4 - Property + Ownership

25. Bundle of Rights

Ownership is a set of legal rights: possession, control, enjoyment, exclusion, and disposition.

Exam rule: Government powers (tax, eminent domain, police power, escheat) can limit the bundle.
"Ownership is a bundle of rights that can be shared, leased, or limited."

26. Real vs Personal Property

Real property: land and things permanently attached. Personal property: movable items.

Exam rule: Contracts should state what conveys. Do not assume appliances convey.
Trap: Assuming a freestanding fridge automatically stays with the house.

27. Fixtures

A fixture is personal property that became real property through attachment or adaptation.

  • Method of attachment
  • Adaptation
  • Intent
  • Agreement of the parties
Exam rule: When clear, written agreement controls.

28. Estates in Land

Freehold = ownership (fee simple, defeasible, life estate). Leasehold = possession/use for a term (estate for years, periodic, at will, at sufferance).

Exam rule: Leasehold is possession, not ownership.
"Freehold owns. Leasehold possesses."

29. Forms of Ownership

  • Severalty: one owner
  • Tenancy in common: undivided interests (can be unequal); no automatic survivorship
  • Joint tenancy: equal shares + survivorship; four unities
  • Tenancy by the entirety: married couple form in NY with survivorship
Trap: Assuming every co-ownership has survivorship. Tenancy in common does not.
NY note: Tenancy by the entirety matters on marital ownership questions.

30. Condos

Condo owners own their unit plus an undivided interest in common elements. Common charges fund operations.

Exam rule: Condo = real property ownership of a unit (plus common interest).
"Condo deed for the box you live in, plus shared building ownership."

31. Co-ops

Co-op buyers own shares in a corporation and receive a proprietary lease.

Exam rule: Co-op interest is primarily personal property (shares) plus lease rights - not fee simple ownership of the apartment unit.
NY note: Manhattan has a large co-op market. Exam focuses on ownership structure more than board politics.
"Condo = real property unit. Co-op = shares plus proprietary lease."

32. Easements + Encumbrances

An easement is a right to use another's land for a specific purpose. An encumbrance affects title or use (liens, easements, covenants).

  • Appurtenant benefits land; in gross benefits a person/utility
  • Creation: grant, necessity, prescription (often ~10 years in NY), implication, condemnation
  • License is revocable permission - not an easement
Trap: Calling a revocable license an easement.
Volume 5 - Contracts + Transactions

33. Contract Elements

A contract is a legally enforceable agreement.

  • Offer
  • Acceptance
  • Consideration
  • Legal purpose
  • Competent parties
  • Mutual consent
Exam rule: Missing an essential can destroy enforceability.
"A contract is not a vibe. It is enforceable architecture."

34. Offer and Acceptance

Acceptance agrees to exact terms. Changing terms is a counteroffer, which rejects the original offer and creates a new one.

Trap: Calling a signed counteroffer an acceptance of the first offer.
"Exact terms = acceptance. Changed terms = new deal."

35. Consideration

Consideration is something of legal value exchanged - money, a promise, or performance.

Exam rule: It need not be large, but it must exist. Mutual promises can be consideration; earnest money is not the only form.

36. Valid / Void / Voidable

  • Valid: enforceable
  • Void: no legal effect (illegal purpose)
  • Voidable: one party may cancel due to a legal defect (often a minor)
  • Unenforceable: exists but cannot be enforced due to a defense (oral real estate sale under Statute of Frauds)
Trap: Calling a minor's contract automatically void instead of voidable.
"Void = dead. Voidable = one side holds a cancel button."

37. Listing Agreements

  • Exclusive right to sell: strongest - commission if sold during term even if owner finds buyer
  • Exclusive agency: owner may sell personally without owing listing broker
  • Open listing: only procuring cause earns
  • Net listing: illegal in New York
NY note: Net listings are illegal. Exclusive right to sell is the exam favorite.

38. Purchase Contracts

Sets price, parties, property description, deposit, financing, closing date, contingencies, and inclusions.

Exam rule: Real estate sale contracts generally must be in writing (Statute of Frauds).
NY note: Attorney-driven culture; attorney approval contingencies are common in practice.

39. Contingencies

A contingency is a condition that must be satisfied for obligations to proceed.

  • Financing / mortgage
  • Inspection
  • Appraisal
  • Title
  • Sale of buyer's home
  • Attorney approval (common in NY residential)
"A contingency is an escape hatch if a specific condition fails."

40. Breach and Remedies

Breach is failure to perform a contractual duty.

  • Damages: money
  • Liquidated damages: pre-agreed amount (often deposit logic)
  • Specific performance: court orders performance (powerful because real estate is unique)
  • Rescission: cancel and unwind
Exam rule: Specific performance is a classic remedy when a seller refuses to convey under a valid contract.
Trap: Thinking the only remedy is keeping the deposit.
Volume 6 - Finance + Mortgages

41. Mortgage Basics

Note = promise to repay. Mortgage = security instrument / lien on the property.

NY note: NY uses mortgages, not deeds of trust as the primary instrument.
Trap: Calling the mortgage the promise to pay.

42. Notes and Liens

A lien is a claim against property to secure a debt.

  • Voluntary: mortgage
  • Involuntary: tax, judgment, mechanic's
  • Specific: one property (mortgage, property tax, mechanic's)
  • General: all property (judgment; many federal tax liens)
Trap: Calling a property tax lien a general lien. It is specific (and often has special priority).

43. Interest

Interest is the cost of borrowing money.

Interest = Principal × Rate × Time

Convert percent to decimal and match time (year vs months as a fraction of a year).

Example: $100,000 × 6% × 1 year = $6,000. For 3 months: $100,000 × .06 × (3/12) = $1,500.

Trap: Using 6 instead of 0.06, or forgetting to prorate months.

44. Points

A point is a loan fee / prepaid interest equal to 1% of the loan amount.

1 point = 1% of loan amount

Example: $400,000 loan → 1 point = $4,000; 2 points = $8,000.

Trap: Multiplying points by purchase price instead of loan amount.

45. LTV

LTV = Loan Amount ÷ Purchase Price (or appraised value if the question says so)

Example: $800,000 loan / $1,000,000 price = 80% LTV; down payment 20%.

Higher LTV means less equity. PMI often appears when conventional LTV is high.

Trap: Flipping the fraction (price ÷ loan).

46. Amortization

Amortization pays a loan down over time through scheduled payments.

  • Fully amortized: balance reaches zero by term end if paid as agreed
  • Interest-only: payment covers interest; principal stays
  • Negative amortization: balance rises because payment does not cover interest
  • Balloon: large final payment

47. Conventional / FHA / VA

  • Conventional: not insured/guaranteed by the federal government
  • FHA: government-insured program
  • VA: benefit for eligible veterans / service members
  • USDA: certain rural eligibility programs
Trap: Saying FHA loans are "made by the government" rather than insured (wording matters).
Exam rule: Know broad differences, not deep underwriting.

48. Foreclosure Basics

Foreclosure is the legal process where a lender enforces a lien after borrower default.

  • Default
  • Acceleration (whole balance due)
  • Notice / sale
  • Auction
  • Deficiency / redemption where applicable
"Default breaks the promise. Foreclosure enforces the collateral."
Volume 7 - Valuation + Appraisal

49. Market Value

Most probable price a willing buyer and seller would agree to in an open market, both informed and not under duress.

Trap: Equating list price, assessed value, or sentimental value with market value.

50. CMA vs Appraisal

CMA: licensee tool using comps for pricing strategy - not an appraisal. Appraisal: formal opinion of value by an appraiser under USPAP.

Trap: Calling a CMA an appraisal in advertising.

51. Sales Comparison Approach

Compare the subject to similar recent sold properties and adjust comps for differences. Best for residential with good comps.

Trap: Using asking prices as if they were sold comps.

52. Cost Approach

Value = Land value + Cost new - Depreciation

Best for new construction or special-purpose properties with weak comps.

53. Income Approach

Value comes from the income stream. Best for income-producing property.

"Price the cash flow, not the paint color."

54. NOI

NOI = Effective gross income - Operating expenses
Trap: Subtracting mortgage debt service inside NOI. Financing is after NOI.

55. Cap Rate

Cap Rate = NOI ÷ Value
Value = NOI ÷ Cap Rate
NOI = Value × Cap Rate

Example: NOI $100,000 / 5% = Value $2,000,000. If NOI is fixed, higher cap rate means lower value.

Trap: Dividing value by NOI when asked for cap rate.

56. Depreciation

Depreciation is loss in value from any cause in cost-approach thinking.

  • Physical deterioration: wear and tear
  • Functional obsolescence: outdated design / poor layout
  • External / economic obsolescence: outside forces (nuisance, nearby decline)
Exam rule: External obsolescence is generally incurable by the owner alone.
Trap: Calling a bad floor plan physical deterioration.
Volume 8 - Land Use + Environmental

57. Police Power

Government power to regulate for health, safety, morals, and general welfare: zoning, building codes, environmental rules, rent regulations.

Trap: Calling zoning eminent domain. Police power regulates; eminent domain takes with compensation.

58. Zoning

Zoning controls land use and intensity. Ownership does not equal unlimited use.

Trap: Assuming a purchased lot can become anything the buyer wants.
"Title says you own it. Zoning says what you may do with it."

59. Variances

A variance is permission to deviate from zoning rules. Area variance = dimensional relief. Use variance = otherwise illegal use (harder).

Exam rule: A variance is an exception, not a rezoning.

60. Nonconforming Use

A use that was legal before zoning changed and no longer conforms, often allowed to continue under limits.

Trap: Thinking any illegal use is nonconforming. Nonconforming started legal.

61. Building Codes

Building codes set construction and safety standards under police power. Certificates of Occupancy relate to lawful occupancy.

Trap: Ignoring that unpermitted work can create closing, lender, and insurer problems.

62. Eminent Domain

Government takes private property for public use with just compensation. Condemnation is the process.

Trap: Confusing eminent domain (take + pay) with police power (regulate).

63. Environmental Hazards

  • Lead-based paint
  • Asbestos
  • Radon
  • Mold
  • Underground storage tanks
  • Wetlands / contamination
Exam rule: Disclose known material hazards. Recommend specialists. Do not play scientist.

64. Lead Paint

Pre-1978 housing has special federal lead-based paint disclosure rules.

Exam rule: Memorize 1978 as the trigger year. Do not ignore lead disclosure on older housing.
Volume 9 - Title + Closing

65. Deeds

A deed transfers ownership interest from grantor to grantee.

  • Essentials: competent grantor, identifiable grantee, consideration (often recited), words of conveyance, legal description, grantor signature, delivery and acceptance
  • Protection high to low: full covenant and warranty · bargain and sale (common in NY) · quitclaim (no warranties)
Exam rule: Title passes on delivery with intent. Recording gives constructive notice; it does not create the transfer by itself.
NY note: Bargain and sale deeds are common in NY residential practice. Executor's / referee's deeds appear in estate or court contexts.
Trap: Thinking an unrecorded deed cannot transfer title between the parties.

66. Title

Title is the legal right/evidence of ownership. A title search reviews public records. Title insurance covers certain defects.

Trap: Assuming a deed always means clean title with no liens or gaps.
"A deed moves interest. Title quality asks whether that interest is clean."

67. Recording

Recording in the county where the property lies gives constructive notice and helps priority.

Trap: Recording does not cure an invalid deed.

68. Liens

  • Mortgage = voluntary specific
  • Property tax = involuntary specific (special priority often)
  • Mechanic's = involuntary specific
  • Judgment = involuntary general
Trap: Assuming a first mortgage always beats unpaid property taxes.

69. Taxes

Property taxes are ad valorem (based on assessed value). Special assessments fund specific improvements.

NY note: Transfer taxes and NYC mansion tax can appear on higher-price deals - know they exist; exact brackets change.
Trap: Using market value as assessed value when an assessment ratio is given.

70. Closing Statement

Shows debits and credits to buyer and seller.

Debit = charge · Credit = benefit / amount already paid or received

Earnest money already paid often appears as a buyer credit.

Trap: Flipping debit and credit.

71. Proration

Divides shared expenses between buyer and seller based on closing date: taxes, rents, common charges, interest, sometimes fuel/oil.

Exam rule: Read whether the item was paid in advance or in arrears before assigning debit/credit.

72. Escrow

Neutral holding of money or documents until conditions are met.

Exam rule: Protect, account for, and release funds only under proper authority. Mixing = commingling. Misuse = conversion.
Volume 10 - Math Mastery

73. Commission Math

Commission = Sale Price × Rate

Example: $800,000 × 6% = $48,000. If 50/50 sides = $24,000. If agent earns 70% of side = $16,800 gross before taxes/fees.

Trap: Applying the agent split to the wrong base amount.

74. Loan Math

Down payment = Price - Loan
LTV = Loan ÷ Price
Loan = Price × LTV

Example: Price $500,000, loan $400,000 → LTV 80%, down payment $100,000 (20%).

Trap: Mixing loan percent with down-payment percent.

75. Interest Math

Interest = Principal × Rate × Time

$200,000 × 6% = $12,000 annual. Monthly simple version: $12,000 ÷ 12 = $1,000. For 6 months: multiply annual by 6/12.

76. Proration Math

  1. Find the annual (or monthly) amount
  2. Divide by 12 or 365/360 as directed
  3. Count days/months owed by each party
  4. Debit one party and credit the other
Trap: Using 365 when the problem says banker's 360, or ignoring advance vs arrears.

77. Area Math

Area = length × width
1 acre = 43,560 square feet

Example: 100 × 200 = 20,000 sq ft.

Trap: Memorizing 43,500 or 45,360. The number is 43,560.

78. Tax Math

Tax = Assessed Value × Tax Rate
Assessed Value = Market Value × Assessment Ratio (when given)

Example: Assessed $400,000 × 2% = $8,000 annual tax.

Trap: Skipping the assessment ratio when provided.

79. Cap Rate Math

Cap Rate = NOI ÷ Value
Value = NOI ÷ Cap Rate
NOI = Value × Cap Rate

Example: NOI $150,000 / 5% = Value $3,000,000.

Trap: Using gross rent instead of NOI when expenses are given.

80. Exam Math Traps

  • Percent not converted to decimal
  • Months not converted to fraction of year
  • Using sale price instead of loan amount for points
  • Confusing debit and credit
  • Including debt service in NOI
  • Forgetting 1 acre = 43,560 sq ft
  • Misreading which split percentage applies
Exam rule: Underline unit words: loan vs price, annual vs monthly, NOI vs gross, advance vs arrears.
"Slow down for units. Speed is how smart people miss easy points."

  • Buyer tells listing agent max price - listing agent owes loyalty to seller; buyer is not owed confidentiality unless they are a client.
  • Dual agency needs informed consent / disclosure.
  • Duty that continues after agency ends: confidentiality.
  • §443 at first substantive contact. PCD or $500 credit for many 1-4 family sales.
  • Net listing illegal in NY. Bargain and sale deed common in NY.

Fair housing

  • "Perfect for young families" - familial status / steering risk.
  • Seller refuses buyers of a religion - refuse illegal instruction.
  • "Is it safe?" - point to objective public data; do not characterize safety.

Contracts / finance / property / valuation / title

  • Seller changes price and signs = counteroffer.
  • Illegal purpose = void. Minor often = voidable.
  • 1 point on $300,000 = $3,000. $400k/$500k = 80% LTV.
  • Note = promise; mortgage = security. Condo = unit; co-op = shares + proprietary lease.
  • Residential best approach = sales comparison. Income property = income approach. Cap rate = NOI ÷ value.
  • Grantor gives; grantee receives. Recording gives public notice / priority.
Exam trap dictionary

Word triggers

  • "Best" - safest legal answer, not fastest business answer
  • "May" - permissive; read carefully
  • "Must" - legal requirement; look for disclosure, writing, broker, escrow, fair housing
  • "Directly paid to salesperson" - usually suspicious
  • "Neighborhood character" - fair housing risk
  • "Guaranteed value increase" - misrepresentation risk
  • "Verbal agreement" - Statute of Frauds issue for real estate sales

Glossary

Exam terms (requested set)

Plain-English definitions for high-frequency license-exam vocabulary. Confirm nuances against your current DOS / course materials.

Transfer of Development Rights (TDR)
A land-use tool that lets unused development rights (density/floor area) from one parcel be transferred to another parcel, often to preserve landmarks or open space while allowing growth elsewhere.
Land use
Triple Net Lease
A commercial lease where the tenant pays base rent plus property taxes, insurance, and maintenance (the three "nets"). Landlord expenses are largely shifted to the tenant.
Leases
Trustee
The party who holds legal title to property in a trust for the benefit of another (the beneficiary). In deed-of-trust states, the trustee holds title as security until the loan is paid; NY primarily uses mortgages instead.
Ownership / finance
Trustor
The party who creates a trust and transfers property into it (also called the settlor/grantor of the trust). In deed-of-trust loan language, the borrower is often called the trustor.
Ownership / finance
Umbrella Policy
An insurance policy that provides extra liability coverage above the limits of underlying policies (home, auto, etc.). It sits over primary coverage for catastrophic claims.
Insurance
Underground Storage Tanks (USTs)
Tanks and piping buried underground that store petroleum or hazardous substances. Leak risk creates environmental liability, disclosure, and remediation issues on real property.
Environmental
Underwriting
The lender's (or insurer's) process of evaluating risk before approving a loan or policy - credit, income, assets, property, and loan program guidelines.
Finance
Undisclosed Dual Agency
Representing both sides (or acting as if) without required disclosure and informed consent. Illegal / severely improper. Dual agency, where allowed, must be disclosed and consented to.
Agency
Undivided Interest
Ownership of a fractional share in the whole property, not a physically divided piece. Classic in tenancy in common and joint tenancy: each owner has rights in the entire parcel.
Ownership
Undivided Loyalty
The fiduciary duty to put one client's interests first without split allegiance. Dual agency limits full undivided loyalty to both sides at once - that is why consent/disclosure matters.
Agency
Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
A body of standardized commercial law adopted (with variations) by states. In real estate practice, UCC filings often relate to personal property / fixtures / business assets used as collateral, not the real-property deed system itself.
Law / finance
Unities of Interest, Possession, Time and Title
The four unities traditionally required for joint tenancy: same interest, same possession rights, acquired at the same time, on the same title. If a unity is broken, joint tenancy may convert toward tenancy in common.
Ownership
Urea Formaldehyde Foam Insulation (UFFI)
An older foam insulation that can release formaldehyde gas. Environmental / disclosure concern in some older buildings; testing and remediation may be issues in diligence.
Environmental
Usable (Useable) Square Footage
The space a tenant can actually occupy and use - typically excluding shared common areas counted in rentable square footage. Rentable SF is usually larger than usable SF.
Leases / measurement
Use Variance
Zoning relief allowing a use otherwise prohibited in the district (harder to get than an area variance). Distinct from an area variance (dimensional relief) and from rezoning.
Land use
Usury
Charging interest above the legal maximum rate. Usurious loans can face penalties or unenforceability under applicable law. Exam focus: illegal excessive interest.
Finance / law
VA Mortgage
A mortgage loan program for eligible veterans and service members, typically guaranteed (not conventional). Often associated with favorable down-payment features for qualified borrowers.
Finance
Valuation
The process of estimating worth. Includes appraisal approaches (sales comparison, cost, income) and related tools like CMAs. Result is an opinion/estimate, not a guaranteed future price.
Appraisal
Value-in-Use
Value of a property to a particular user for a specific use - not necessarily the same as market value to a typical buyer. Special-purpose or owner-specific utility can drive value-in-use.
Appraisal
Variable Expense
An operating expense that changes with occupancy or usage (utilities, some maintenance, management tied to collections). Contrast with fixed expenses that stay relatively stable.
Appraisal / income
Variance
Permission from a zoning board to deviate from zoning requirements. Includes area variances (dimensions) and use variances (prohibited uses). A variance is an exception, not a rezoning.
Land use
Vicarious Liability
Legal responsibility for the acts of another. In brokerage, a broker may have liability for a salesperson's licensed activity under supervision rules - a reason supervision and training matter.
Brokerage / law
Village Board of Trustees
The governing legislative body of a village in New York local government. May handle local laws, budgeting, and appointments; land-use roles interact with planning/zoning boards depending on locality.
Government / land use
Violation
A breach of law, code, regulation, or license rule (building code violation, zoning violation, license law violation). Can create fines, stop-work orders, closing issues, or disciplinary action.
Law / compliance
Void
Having no legal effect from the start. A void contract is not enforceable (example: agreement with an illegal purpose). Different from voidable, which one party may cancel.
Contracts

Practice - Licensing + Broker Supervision

Q1 · Licensing + Broker Supervision
A licensed salesperson wants to open their own office and collect commissions directly from buyers. What is the issue?
  • A. Allowed if the salesperson has completed the course
  • B. Allowed if the buyer agrees in writing
  • C. Not allowed because a salesperson must act through a sponsoring broker
  • D. Allowed only for residential property
Show answer

Answer: C

A salesperson must work under broker supervision. Compensation generally flows through the broker.

Trap: Written consent from a buyer does not override licensing law.

Q2 · Licensing + Broker Supervision
An owner sells their own house without a real estate license. Is that allowed?
  • A. No, all sellers need a license
  • B. Yes, owners may usually sell their own property
  • C. Only if they use a broker
  • D. Only if the house is vacant
Show answer

Answer: B

Licensing is usually required when acting for another person for compensation.

Answer: C

The issue is broker supervision and compensation structure, not the dollar amount.
