11 branches Decision trees Fiduciary duties always on
1

People

Relationships, agency, listings, disclosure, licensing

1.1 Agency

  • NY types: seller's agent, buyer's agent, broker's agent, dual agent, designated sales agent (under disclosed dual agency)
  • Creation: express (written or oral) or implied by conduct; listing agreements that create agency should be in writing
  • Termination: fulfillment, mutual agreement, expiration; operation of law (death, incapacity, bankruptcy, destruction of the property); acts of the parties (revocation, renunciation, abandonment)

1.2 Fiduciary duties (OLD CAR)

  • Obedience · Loyalty · Disclosure
  • Confidentiality · Accounting · Reasonable care (includes skill and diligence)
  • Also obey license law and fair housing. Confidentiality continues after the relationship ends for client secrets.
Loyalty = client's interest first Accounting = money and documents

1.3 Listings & buyer agreements

  • Exclusive right to sell: listing broker is owed a commission if the property sells during the term, even if the owner finds the buyer
  • Exclusive agency: owner may sell personally without owing the listing broker
  • Open listing: many brokers; only the procuring cause earns
  • Net listing: illegal in New York (broker keeps anything above a net price to the seller)
  • Buyer representation: exclusive or non-exclusive buyer agency agreement

1.4 Disclosure (NY focus)

  • Real Property Law §443 agency disclosure: seller/buyer and landlord/tenant forms; provide at first substantive contact
  • Property Condition Disclosure: NY sellers of most 1-4 family homes must give a PCD statement or credit the buyer $500
  • Disclose known material facts; do not lie or conceal known defects. Stigmatized-property rules are not the same as physical defects

1.5 Licensing & practice

  • DOS / DLS: Division of Licensing Services, New York Department of State
  • Categories: salesperson, associate broker, broker; pocket card; salesperson must be sponsored by a broker
  • Independent contractor vs employee: written IC agreement is common; the broker still supervises licensees
  • Commission: always negotiable; paid through the broker; referral fees require proper licensing and agreements
2

Ownership (Estates & Interests)

Who owns what, and how long

2.1 Bundle of rights

  • Possess · Control · Enjoy · Exclude · Dispose · Devise / bequeath

2.2 Estates in real property

  • Freehold (ownership): fee simple absolute, fee simple defeasible (condition / determinable), life estate
  • Leasehold (possession): estate for years (fixed term), periodic tenancy, tenancy at will, tenancy at sufferance (holdover)

2.3 Forms of ownership

  • Severalty: one owner
  • Tenancy in common: undivided interests (can be unequal); no right of survivorship; heirs take
  • Joint tenancy: equal shares + right of survivorship; four unities (time, title, interest, possession)
  • Tenancy by the entirety: married couple (NY); survivorship; neither alone can convey whole

2.4 Related structures

  • Trusts; life estate / remainder / reversion
  • Business entities: LLC, corporation, partnership (entity holds title)
  • Condo = real property (unit + undivided common interest). Co-op = shares of stock + proprietary lease (personal property interest in the shares)
3

Rights on Land

Encumbrances & limitations on ownership

3.1 Easements

  • Appurtenant: benefits a dominant estate; burdens a servient estate; runs with the land
  • In gross: benefits a person or utility; no dominant estate
  • Creation: grant (written), necessity (landlocked), prescription (open, notorious, continuous, hostile = without permission; commonly 10 years in NY), implication, condemnation
  • Termination: release, abandonment, merger of dominant and servient, condemnation, end of purpose / prescription against the easement

3.2 Encroachments & surveys

  • Physical intrusion across a boundary (fence, building overhang, driveway). Surveys and legal descriptions settle location disputes

3.3 Liens

  • Voluntary: mortgage created by the owner (NY uses mortgages, not deeds of trust)
  • Involuntary: judgment, mechanic's / materialman's, property tax, federal or state tax, child support (created by law or court process)
  • Specific: attaches to one property (mortgage, property tax, mechanic's)
  • General: attaches to all of the debtor's property (judgment lien; many federal tax liens)
  • Lis pendens: recorded notice of pending litigation about the property - not itself a money lien

3.4 Deed restrictions & appurtenances

  • Covenants / deed restrictions: affirmative or negative; may run with the land
  • Appurtenances: rights, privileges, and improvements that transfer with the land
  • License (revocable permission, not an estate); profit a prendre; mineral rights; riparian (flowing water) / littoral (lake or ocean); avulsion (sudden) vs accretion (gradual)
4

Transferring Property

Title, deeds, closing, recording

4.1 Deeds (conveyance)

  • Types (protection high to low): full covenant and warranty · bargain and sale (most common in NY residential) · quitclaim (no warranties of title)
  • Also: executor's, administrator's, referee's when estate or court sale context requires
  • Essentials: competent grantor, identifiable grantee, consideration (often recited), granting words, legal description, grantor signature, delivery and acceptance. Acknowledgment needed to record
  • Title passes on delivery with intent to convey - recording gives constructive notice; it is not what transfers title

4.2 Title

  • Chain of title · public records search · abstract · title report · owner's / lender's title insurance

4.3 Closing process & money

  • Typical path: signed contract → title work → loan approval → appraisal → Closing Disclosure → closing → recording
  • Roles: attorneys (NY attorney-closing culture), title company, lender, brokers
  • Buyer costs: points, title insurance, recording, mortgage tax (where applicable), prepaids
  • Seller costs: commission, transfer tax, mortgage payoff, prorations
  • Adjustments: taxes, rents, common charges, fuel/oil

4.4 Recording

  • Gives constructive notice; priority among recorded interests; record in the county where the property lies
5

Contracts & Leases

Promises that bind parties

5.1 Essentials

  • Offer · acceptance · consideration · legal capacity · lawful purpose · writing when Statute of Frauds applies (real property sales)

5.2 Contingencies

  • Financing · appraisal · home sale · inspection · attorney approval (common in NY residential)

5.3 Leases

  • Types: gross, net / triple-net, percentage (retail)
  • Elements: parties, premises, term, rent, use, default, renewal / options

5.4 Changes & breach

  • Addenda · amendments · assignment · novation
  • Breach remedies: specific performance, damages, rescission, forfeiture of deposit (per contract / law)
6

Money & Financing

How purchases get funded

6.1 Structures & products

  • Fixed · ARM · balloon · interest-only · fully amortizing
  • Conventional · FHA · VA · USDA · jumbo

6.2 Key terms

  • PITI · LTV · PMI · DTI · points / discount points
  • Loan costs, third-party fees, prepaids, escrow / reserves

6.3 Taxes touching the deal

  • Ad valorem property tax · assessment · tax liens · NYS / local transfer taxes · NYC mansion tax on high-price residential purchases where it applies
7

Value

Worth, price, and appraisal

7.1 CMA vs appraisal

  • CMA: licensee tool using comps / adjustments for listing or offer strategy - not an appraisal
  • Appraisal: licensed / certified appraiser; USPAP; report for lender or other client

7.2 Approaches to value

  • Sales comparison · Cost (replacement / reproduction minus depreciation) · Income (cap rate / GRM)
8

Land Use & Zoning

What you can build and how

8.1 Zoning & approvals

  • Zoning map · districts · use regs · bulk / dimensional regs
  • Area variance vs use variance · special / conditional use permit
  • Planning board · site plan · subdivision · SEQRA / environmental review
  • Building permit · Certificate of Occupancy (CO) · violations
9

Buildings & Structures

Physical property & systems

9.1 Structure, systems, safety

  • Foundation · framing · roof · walls · floors
  • Plumbing · electrical · heating / cooling / HVAC
  • Fire safety · exits · smoke / CO detectors · handrails · code compliance
  • Inspections: home · WDI / termite · radon · oil tank
10

Environmental Issues

Hazards, laws, disclosure

10.1 Hazards & law

  • Lead-based paint (pre-1978 disclosure) · asbestos · mold · radon · underground oil tanks · meth lab residue
  • CERCLA / Superfund · brownfields · Clean Water Act · NYS environmental rules
  • Disclose known environmental defects; property condition forms
11

Fair Housing & Law

Who is protected and what is banned

11.1 Federal + New York

  • Federal Fair Housing Act: race, color, religion, national origin, sex, disability, familial status
  • NY Human Rights Law adds (among others): age, marital status, military status, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, lawful source of income, and additional classes - memorize the current DOS exam list for your year
  • Illegal practices: refusal to deal, steering, blockbusting, redlining, disparate treatment, disparate impact
  • Advertising must not express preference, limitation, or discrimination based on a protected class

Decision trees

Easements

How was it created?
Written grant → Easement by grant
Landlocked → Necessity
Long open hostile use (w/o permission; often 10 yrs NY) → Prescription
Prior common ownership / intent → Implication
Government takes for public use → Condemnation

Liens

Who created it?
Owner (mortgage) → Voluntary
Law / court / statute → Involuntary
What does it attach to?
One property → Specific
All property → General

Deeds

How much protection?
WarrantyMost Bargain & saleNY common QuitclaimNone

Ownership

Who owns?
One person / entity → Severalty
Two+ · no survivorship → Tenancy in common
Two+ · equal · survivorship → Joint tenancy
Married couple (NY) → Tenancy by the entirety

Agency

Who does the agent represent?
Seller → Listing / seller's agent
Buyer → Buyer's agent
Both (disclosed) → Dual agent
Same firm, dual agency, different agents → Designated sales agent
Remember fiduciary duties at all times.
