Field guide

How to buy in NYC

New York runs on attorneys, not escrow theater. Choose the path, know the twelve steps, budget day-one and carry, then decide where you will not stall.

Path chooser

Pick the building type first

Condo, co-op, and house each change the paperwork, the board (or lack of one), and what you carry after closing. A 1031 is not a fourth type — it is an overlay on a sale that funds a purchase.

Deed

Condo

You buy a unit (and a share of common elements) with a deed. Title insurance, mansion tax, and — if financed — mortgage recording tax sit on the buyer’s side. Boards exist, but they do not interview like a co-op.

Shares + lease

Co-op

You buy shares and a proprietary lease. No deed transfer, so no title policy and no mortgage recording tax in the condo sense — but the board package, interview, and possible PASS decide whether the deal lives.

Rare in Manhattan volume

House / townhouse

Fee simple, land and structure — inspections matter. Thin on this page because the volume is condo and co-op. Separate guide next.

Overlay — not a building type

1031 exchange

If you are selling investment property and buying another, a qualified intermediary and the 45 / 180-day clocks sit on top of the same sell → buy sequence. Choose condo, co-op, or house as the asset; the exchange rules decide the timing and the QI, not the floor plan.

After the keys

What you carry once you own

Day-one costs get you to closing. Carry decides whether the home still fits six months later. Ask for the monthly number before you lock the offer.

Condo — common charges

Monthly common charges cover building operations, reserves, and shared services. Real-estate taxes are billed separately (often escrowed with the loan). Read the budget and the minutes for special assessments already in motion.

Co-op — maintenance

Maintenance usually wraps building ops and the shareholder’s share of the underlying mortgage and taxes into one line. Compare buildings on all-in monthly cost, not just share price.

House / townhouse — taxes & systems

You own the envelope. Property taxes, insurance, and every boiler, roof, and sidewalk repair sit with you. An inspection is not optional theater — it is how you price carry.

Friction

Where deals stall

Most NYC files do not die on the floor plan. They die on funds, boards, and paper that arrives late from abroad.

Source of funds

WAIT

Money that appears from an account nobody has documented stops the file at the bank, the title company, or both. Show where the funds sit and how they got there at the start — not when the wire is due. Decision stamp: incomplete trail = WAIT until the paper is clean.

Co-op board

PASS risk

A board can decline without giving a reason. Thin packages — light U.S. income history, thin references, or assets that do not read clearly on the application — raise PASS risk after the deposit is already committed. Build the package with counsel before you romanticize the lobby.

Signing from abroad / POA

CONSIDER

Contracts can be signed remotely, but a power of attorney must be drafted, notarized, and often apostilled in your country. That is measured in weeks, not days. Start it early, or CONSIDER delaying the offer until the POA path is real.

How you hold title

PICK early

Personally, through a U.S. LLC, or another structure changes income tax, estate exposure, and what happens on sale. PICK the form with counsel and your CPA before contract — restructuring after is expensive theater.

Short answers

FAQ

Can a non-resident buy U.S. property?

Yes. There is no citizenship or residency requirement to own real property in the United States, and owning it does not confer a visa.

Do I need to be in New York to close?

No. Closings are routinely completed by power of attorney. Remote notarization is available in some circumstances. The POA must be prepared well in advance.

How large a deposit is normal?

Ten percent of the price at contract, held in the seller attorney’s escrow until closing. It is at risk if you walk away outside the contract’s contingencies.

Is an inspection usual in New York?

Less common for apartments than elsewhere in the U.S., because many building systems are the building’s responsibility. For a house or townhouse it is essential. For a large apartment it is still often worth doing.
