Here’s a ready-to-paste training prompt you can feed into your website builder / AI page generator. It’s written as if you are instructing the AI to build the page for your site.

You can tweak the name (“Agent Kammer”) or any details, but this should give you a clean, numbered-tab flow for international buyers focused on California.

⸻


You are designing a customer-facing web page for a luxury real estate agent in California who specializes in helping **international buyers** purchase property.

GOAL:
Create a beautiful, clear, and confidence-building page titled:

“International Buyers Guide – Purchasing Property in California (Step-by-Step)”

This page will be structured as a **numbered tab flow** so an international buyer can click through:
1️⃣ Overview  
2️⃣ The Buying Process (Step-by-Step)  
3️⃣ Financing, Funds & Currency Transfers  
4️⃣ Legal & Tax Basics for Non-US Buyers  
5️⃣ FAQs for International Buyers  
6️⃣ Work With [Agent Name] (Call-to-Action)

STYLE & TONE:
- Tone: warm, competent, concierge-style, NOT legalese. Imagine a very friendly but sharp Beverly Hills / LA real estate concierge explaining things clearly.
- Audience: high-intent international buyers (non-US citizens or non-US residents) considering buying in **California** (Los Angeles, San Diego, Orange County, Bay Area, etc.).
- Reading level: 8th–10th grade. No jargon without quick plain-English explanation.
- Length: Each tab should feel like a mini-guide (short sections, bullets, subheadings), NOT a giant wall of text.
- Always be clear this is **general information**, not tax or legal advice.

PAGE STRUCTURE (TABS):

🟦 TAB 1 – OVERVIEW: “1. Welcome, International Buyers”
Goal: Give a big-picture sense of safety and clarity.

Include:
- A short welcome paragraph to international buyers.
- A simple statement that **you do NOT need to be a US citizen or US resident to buy property in California**.
- Bullet list of common buyer profiles (e.g., overseas parents buying for children, investors, second-home buyers, relocation plans).
- Quick “What’s special about California?” bullets: lifestyle, education, major job centers, long-term demand, etc.
- A mini “What this page will help you do” checklist:
  - Understand the steps to buy
  - Learn how financing and money transfers work
  - Learn the basic legal & tax concepts to ask your advisors about
  - Know how to work with a trusted local agent

Add a short **disclaimer** at the bottom like:
“Nothing on this page is legal, tax, or immigration advice. Always consult your own attorney, CPA, and immigration advisor before making decisions.”

🟦 TAB 2 – PROCESS: “2. The Buying Process – Step-by-Step”
Goal: A clear, numbered path from “interested” to “keys in hand.”

Create a **step-by-step flow** with short explanations. For example:

1) Clarify your goals & budget  
   - Primary residence vs. vacation/2nd home vs. pure investment  
   - Target neighborhoods, price range, property type  

2) Get pre-qualified (or proof of funds)  
   - Explain briefly: some international buyers pay cash with proof of funds; others use specialized lenders offering loans to foreign nationals.  
   - Mention that lenders may ask for: overseas income/employment documentation, bank statements, credit references, larger down-payment, etc.  

3) Work with a local California real estate agent  
   - How the agent helps: market education, property search, virtual tours, offer strategy, negotiation, coordination with escrow, title, inspectors, etc.  

4) Search & tours (in person or virtual)  
   - In-person showings vs. video/FaceTime tours  
   - Short checklist: commute, school district, amenities, HOA rules, zoning.  

5) Making an offer  
   - Explain simply what an “offer” is: price, terms, contingencies (financing, inspection, appraisal).  
   - Emphasize that your agent explains comps and strategy.  

6) Escrow, inspections & due diligence  
   - Define “escrow” in plain English: a neutral third party that holds funds and coordinates the transaction.  
   - Mention inspections (home inspection, termite, etc.) and disclosures.  

7) Closing & taking title  
   - Funds wired to escrow, documents signed (often can be done remotely / via notary, depending on country).  
   - Title/ownership recorded in the buyer’s name, an entity, or trust (as advised by their attorney/CPA).  

Close Tab 2 with a small “Process Snapshot” box:
- Typical escrow length (e.g., ~30 days, can be shorter/longer)
- Typical deposit amounts (e.g., 3% of purchase price in many cases, varies)
- Note that timing can vary based on financing, documentation, and country of origin.

🟦 TAB 3 – FINANCING & MONEY: “3. Financing, Funds & Currency Transfers”
Goal: Explain how international buyers typically handle money without getting too technical.

Include sections:

A) Payment Options  
- Cash buyers with proof of funds (bank statements, letter from bank/private banker).  
- Financing as an international buyer:
  - Some US and international banks offer “foreign national” mortgage programs.
  - Expect: larger down payment (often 30–40% or more), higher documentation requirements (income, assets), and possibly slightly higher interest rates.

B) Documentation Typical Lenders May Ask For  
- Passport and ID  
- Proof of income (employment letter, contracts, business financials)  
- Bank statements (often 2–12 months)  
- International credit references  

C) Currency Transfers & Anti-Money-Laundering Rules (high-level only)  
- Mention that large international wire transfers are normal but must follow banking and anti-money-laundering regulations.  
- Explain that funds usually need to be **seasoned** (sitting in an account for a certain period, depending on lender) and traceable.  

D) Practical Tips  
- Start speaking with a lender or private banker early.  
- Make sure your name appears consistently on all documents (passports, bank accounts, purchase contract).  
- Prepare back-up documents: translations, apostilles, or certifications if your bank/lender requires them.

Always repeat: “Discuss specifics with your lender, CPA, and legal advisor. This is general information only.”

🟦 TAB 4 – LEGAL & TAX BASICS: “4. Legal & Tax Basics for Non-US Buyers”
Goal: Give a simple overview so buyers know what topics to ask professionals about. Do NOT give detailed advice.

Sections:

A) Ownership & Title  
- Non-US citizens can generally hold title to property in their personal name, a company, trust, or other structure, depending on advice from their legal/tax team.  
- Short bullets for each:
  - Personal: simplest, but may have estate/tax implications.  
  - Entity (LLC, company, etc.): may offer privacy or liability benefits but higher complexity.  
  - Trust or other structures: for estate planning and asset protection, guided by professionals.

B) Property Taxes (Local)  
- Briefly explain: California property tax is based on assessed value, with rates that vary by county and district.  
- Mention supplemental taxes, Mello-Roos or special assessments in some communities.  
- Suggest they ask a local property tax professional for an estimate based on price and area.

C) Income Tax & Rental Income  
- If the buyer rents out the property, rental income may be taxable in the US.  
- Non-US owners often file US tax returns and may have to withhold taxes on rental income.  
- Encourage them to speak with a cross-border tax advisor.

D) FIRPTA & Withholding (Seller-Side but Good to Know)  
- Briefly explain that under US law, there is a withholding tax (FIRPTA) when a **foreign person sells** US real estate, and that’s something they should plan for long-term with their advisors.  
- Make it clear this affects the **future sale**, not the initial purchase, but it’s smart to plan ahead.

E) Immigration Status  
- Clarify that **owning property in California does NOT by itself give a visa, green card, or right to work in the US**.  
- Suggest they speak with an immigration attorney if they are considering residency, business investments, or long-term stays.

End with a bold reminder:
“This section is educational only. Always consult a California real estate attorney, tax advisor, and immigration attorney before buying.”

🟦 TAB 5 – FAQ: “5. Common Questions from International Buyers”
Goal: Rapid-fire, reassurance-style Q&A.

Create 10–15 FAQs with short, clear answers. Example questions:

- “Do I need to be a US citizen or permanent resident to buy a home in California?”  
- “Can I buy a house entirely from overseas without flying to the US?”  
- “Can I put the property in my company’s name?”  
- “How much money do I typically need for a down payment as a foreign national?”  
- “Can I get a mortgage without US credit history?”  
- “How do I move large amounts of money into the US safely?”  
- “What happens with property taxes if I don’t live in the US full-time?”  
- “Can I buy a home for my child who is studying in California?”  
- “Can I rent out the property when I’m not using it?”  
- “How long does the whole purchase process usually take?”  

Each answer should:
- Be 2–4 sentences max.  
- Reassure the buyer and clearly point to the professionals they should speak to when it’s a legal/tax item.  

🟦 TAB 6 – WORK WITH THE AGENT: “6. Work With [Agent Name] – Your International Buyer Concierge”
Goal: Turn this into a soft but clear call-to-action.

Include:
- A short, confident paragraph positioning the agent as an **international-friendly concierge** who is used to working across time zones, with attorneys, CPAs, and private bankers.  
- Bullet list of what the agent does for international buyers:
  - Curated property lists tailored to your goals  
  - Virtual tours with detailed commentary  
  - Coordination with lenders, escrow, title, and inspectors  
  - Introductions to trusted local attorneys, CPAs, and other professionals (as allowed by law)  
  - Ongoing support before, during, and after closing  

Add a simple **3-step CTA**:
1) Fill out a short intake form (budget, timing, goals, country of residence).  
2) Schedule a private Zoom / WhatsApp call.  
3) Receive a tailored California property roadmap and property matches.

End with a warm closing sentence that encourages them to reach out even if they’re just exploring.

DESIGN NOTES:
- Use clear headings, numbered sections, and bullet lists so buyers can skim.  
- Use calm, premium aesthetics (white space, clean typography, subtle accents).  
- On mobile, tabs can stack as an accordion with numbers clearly visible: “1. Overview”, “2. The Buying Process”, etc.

IMPORTANT:
- Do NOT provide specific tax rates, legal conclusions, or immigration guarantees.  
- Always remind the reader to consult licensed professionals for legal, tax, and immigration matters.  
- Keep everything California-specific where relevant, and say “California” often enough that the reader is never confused.
